Pitcher Advance

Before first pitch. Advance scouting on the opposing starter.

Built to get hitters ready for the opposing starter. Pick any MLB pitcher, split him by batter side and go tab by tab: how he attacks, how he sequences, how his pitches tunnel, his release and approach angles, how he holds up deep in games, a video playlist and a printable game plan for the hitters.

Exhibit 1A. Screen recording2:39, no audio

The pitch score model

I built a score for every 2026 pitch type with at least 300 pitches and 40 batted balls. 100 is average and every 10 points is one standard deviation.

  • It combines seven pieces: whiff %, chase %, called-strike %, run value per 100 pitches, xwOBA and xSLG on contact, and a stuff grade.
  • Small samples get pulled toward the league average for that pitch type.
  • The stuff grade measures how different a pitch's movement is from what you'd expect given its velo, extension and arm angle.
  • The weights come from the data. A non-negative ridge regression uses one half of a pitcher's games to predict his run value in the other half, cross-validated by pitcher.

Learned weights and results

On games the model didn't see, the score predicted run value better than run value itself did (r = .223, compared with .127 for RV/100 alone and .196 for the original hand-picked weights). Split-half reliability is .637.

Data: 714,483 pitches from 2,429 games, 99.9% matched between the MLB Stats API and Baseball Savant. 785 qualified pitcher-pitch combinations, ranked overall and by batter side. The pipeline saves every game feed and daily Savant file, checks that the two sources line up and retries failed requests.

Built with R, Shiny and bslib, plotly, DT, ggplot2 and patchwork, glmnet, furrr, httr2, MLB Stats API and Baseball Savant.

Baserunning Review

Reviewing the play. Every runner's route, lead and speed from Hawk-Eye tracking.

Built to grade baserunning off Hawk-Eye tracking. Pick a game and a play, and the app replays every runner's path to scale next to the broadcast video. It measures his leads, his speed and how many extra feet he ran.

Exhibit 2A. Game review1:33
Exhibit 2B. Leaderboard1:04

What it does

  • Play finder for any game at six levels, from MLB down to Rookie ball. Filter by balls in play, stolen base attempts, extra bases taken or pitch by pitch. Steals are tied to the exact pitch they happened on.
  • Route view shows only the runners. Play view adds all nine fielders and the ball, which gets bigger the higher it goes. Both are drawn to scale from 30 frames-per-second tracking.
  • Synced video from every available camera angle.
  • Leaderboard searches a full season by any of 20 running metrics, by play or by player. Clicking a row opens that play in the diagram.
  • Exports a one-page route PDF, a video of the diagram and a CSV of the leaderboard.

Built with R, Shiny, httr2, a custom JavaScript canvas engine, jsPDF and MediaRecorder, on MLB Stats API and Hawk-Eye tracking. About 3,700 lines of R and 1,280 of JavaScript.

How the route is measured

Distance needed
The shortest legal path from where the runner started through every base he had to touch, with the bags placed where the rulebook puts them.
Added feet
The distance he actually ran minus the distance needed, measured while he was moving. The path stops within 4 feet of his final bag, so a wide turn or a walk back isn't held against him.
Leads
Primary lead at the windup, total lead at release and the ground he gained in between.
Derived metrics
First step, acceleration and top speed, calculated from the tracking data where MLB no longer publishes them. Every metric is labeled published or derived.

GameData Pro

The morning after. Team, pitcher and hitter reports from last night's game.

Built for the morning after a game. Load any game and get a team report plus a report for every pitcher and hitter. Click any pitch or batted ball to pull up the video. Every report exports as a print-ready PDF.

Exhibit 3A. Screen recording2:58, no audio

What it does

  • Any game, six levels. Pick a date and a level from MLB down to Rookie ball, and the app loads every pitch. During a live game, Refresh pulls the newest data and rebuilds any open report.
  • Box score that matches the official line. It uses the official scorer's IP, ER, AB and LOB, and handles edge cases like catcher's interference, sac flies in OBP, mid-inning subs and unfinished plate appearances.
  • Team, pitcher and hitter reports with arsenals, true release points, quality of contact, swing decisions and spray charts with pull, center and oppo splits by handedness.
  • Click-to-watch video. Every pitch and batted ball on a chart opens its clip in any available camera angle.
  • Print-ready exports. Each report is a single file laid out for letter paper that saves straight to PDF.

Built with R, Shiny, shinyjs, plotly, ggplot2, DT and HTML/CSS print templates on the MLB Stats API. 4,810 lines of R. It converts the MLB feed into TrackMan-style columns, so the same reports run on MLB data or TrackMan CSVs.
