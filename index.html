Before first pitch · Advance scouting

Pitcher Advance

Built to get hitters ready for the opposing starter. Pick any MLB pitcher, split him by batter side and go tab by tab: how he attacks, how he sequences, how his pitches tunnel, his release and approach angles, how he holds up deep in games, a video playlist and a printable game plan for the hitters.

Overview

Scouting takeaways written automatically: his best pitch with its score and rank, his two-strike pitch, first-pitch mix and his put-away pitch. KPI tiles and an arsenal table shaded against league average.

How he attacks

Pitch mix in every count, plus location heat maps and a 13-zone grid filtered by pitch and outcome.

Sequencing

What he throws next after each pitch, by count, and which pitch pairs get extra whiffs. For example, McLean's cutter after a sinker gets 32% whiffs vs. 18% normally.

Tunneling

Each pitch's average flight from release to the plate, from behind the plate or either batter's box, with the gaps at release, at the commit point and at the plate.

Tunnel video

Two real pitches side by side, or stacked on top of each other and lined up on release, so you can see where they stay together and where they split.

Release

Release points and how consistent each pitch is, ranked against MLB, plus possible tells. For example, his slider comes out 4.1 inches to the side of his sinker.

Approach

Vertical and horizontal approach angles compared with what's expected for his location and velo, a “plays like” velo for fastballs, a grade for each pitch and where in the zone it plays up.

Fatigue

Velo, spin, movement, whiff% and CSW% by pitch count in the outing, plus how hitters do the first, second and third time through the order.

Trends

Velo and pitch usage by outing over the season, and a game log joined to the pitch data.

Clips

Build a video playlist of any pitch filtered by count, result and location. For example, every two-strike four-seam whiff, newest first.

Game plan

A printable one-page plan for hitters: what to take on the first pitch, what to sit on in hitter's counts, what to lay off, how to handle two strikes and where to do damage, with heat maps.

Matchup & Leaderboard

Pitcher vs. a specific hitter with every plate appearance between them, and a leaderboard of the best pitches in baseball.

The pitch score model

I built a score for every 2026 pitch type with at least 300 pitches and 40 batted balls. 100 is average and every 10 points is one standard deviation.

  • It combines seven pieces: whiff %, chase %, called-strike %, run value per 100 pitches, xwOBA and xSLG on contact, and a stuff grade.
  • Small samples get pulled toward the league average for that pitch type.
  • The stuff grade measures how different a pitch's movement is from what you'd expect given its velo, extension and arm angle.
  • The weights come from the data. A non-negative ridge regression uses one half of a pitcher's games to predict his run value in the other half, cross-validated by pitcher.

On games the model didn't see, the score predicted run value better than run value itself did (r = .223, compared with .127 for RV/100 alone and .196 for the original hand-picked weights). Split-half reliability is .637.

Reviewing the play · Hawk-Eye tracking

Baserunning Review

Built to grade baserunning off Hawk-Eye tracking. Pick a game and a play, and the app replays every runner's path to scale next to the broadcast video. It measures his leads, his speed and how many extra feet he ran.

Game Review1:33
Leaderboard1:04

What it does

  • Play finder for any game at six levels, from MLB down to Rookie ball. Filter by balls in play, stolen base attempts, extra bases taken or pitch by pitch. Steals are tied to the exact pitch they happened on.
  • Route view shows only the runners. Play view adds all nine fielders and the ball, which gets bigger the higher it goes. Both are drawn to scale from 30 frames-per-second tracking.
  • Synced video from every available camera angle, in the best quality available.
  • Leaderboard searches a full season by any of 20 running metrics, by play or by player, with filters. Clicking a row opens that play in the diagram.
  • Exports a one-page route PDF, a video of the diagram and a CSV of the leaderboard.

How the route is measured

Distance needed
The shortest legal path from where the runner started through every base he had to touch, with the bags placed where the rulebook puts them.
Added feet
The distance he actually ran minus the distance needed, measured on a smoothed path while he was moving. The path stops within 4 feet of his final bag, so a wide turn or a walk back isn't held against him.
PL / SL / TL
Primary lead at the windup, total lead at release and the ground he gained in between.
Derived first step, acceleration, top speed
Calculated from the tracking data for metrics MLB no longer publishes. Contact is marked at the ball's sharpest change in direction.

Data & build

  • Custom canvas playback engine with play, scrub, frame-by-frame and full-route modes
  • Reads the tracking feed in either of its two layouts and can tell the ball's track apart from a player's

The morning after · Post-game reporting

GameData Pro

Built for the morning after a game. Load any game and get a team report plus a report for every pitcher and hitter. Click any pitch or batted ball to pull up the video. Every report exports as a print-ready PDF.

Game and player reports2:58

What it does

  • Any game, six levels: pick a date and a level from MLB down to Rookie ball, and the app loads every pitch. During a live game, Refresh pulls the newest data and rebuilds any open report.
  • Box score that matches the official line: it uses the official scorer's IP, ER, AB and LOB, and handles edge cases like catcher's interference, sac flies in OBP, mid-inning subs and unfinished plate appearances.
  • Team, pitcher and hitter reports: arsenals with IVB, HB, spin and extension, true release points, quality of contact, swing decisions and spray charts with pull, center and oppo splits by handedness.
  • Click-to-watch video: every pitch and batted ball on a chart opens its clip in any available camera angle. The app remembers the last angle you picked.
  • Print-ready exports: each report is a single file laid out for letter paper, with high-resolution charts, and saves straight to PDF.

Data & build

  • 4,810 lines of R
  • Converts the MLB feed into TrackMan-style columns, so the same reports run on MLB data or TrackMan CSVs
